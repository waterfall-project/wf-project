# Reference budget

## What the glossary says

> **Reference budget** — Designates the sum of the budgeted amounts of the lines of the reference revision, without the provision lines, inflation included, at the year of consumption of the lines. Only an amendment moves it.

> **Budgeted amount** — Designates the amount of an estimate line as the reference revision set it, inflation included, at the year of consumption of the line. It changes only with a new reference, and it is zero for a line added after it.

> **Re-estimated amount** — Designates the amount of an estimate line as the last periodic review estimated it. It is on it that the remaining to commit is calculated.

## In Waterfall

The reference budget is what the project promised: it is born from the designation of a [reference revision](revisions.md) and no longer moves, whatever the reviews, until the next amendment contractualised. A line added after the reference — an unanticipated line — enters it with a zero budgeted amount: it weighs on the remaining to commit, never on the budget.

Two amounts live side by side on each line: the budgeted, which only the reference sets, and the re-estimated, which each review updates. It is their distinction that keeps the drift visible: the margin on the reference budget, in the remaining to commit, and the [cost index](indices.md) measure it against what had been promised, not against the last estimate.

The provision lines of the risks are not part of it: their sum in the reference revision forms the risk reserve, apart.

The reference budget is read in the column “Budgeted amount” of the [remaining to commit](../FBS-4.5.md), and serves as the basis of the [planned value](valeur-planifiee.md) and of the [earned value](valeur-acquise.md).
